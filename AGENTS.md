## What this is

A custom Android default phone app, **One Dialer** (application id `com.syedarhamraza.onedialer`; the Kotlin namespace and channel names stay `com.syedarhamraza.dialer`). The UI is Flutter; a Kotlin layer under `android/` talks to Android's Telecom framework. Android-only (`--platforms android`); `minSdk 29` (needs `RoleManager.ROLE_DIALER`), `compileSdk 37` (required by `permission_handler_android`).

The UI design is ported from the sibling app OneSpend at `D:\one-spend` (pitch-black Samsung One UI); the reference screenshots of Samsung's Phone app are in `Images/` (local only, git-ignored). OneSpend widgets are copied **verbatim** into `lib/widgets/` (reformatted only) and compile against three stand-ins at the same import paths: `lib/theme/one_ui_effects.dart` (`OneUIEffectsController` returning OneSpend's flat-look values, re-exported by `one_ui_theme.dart`), `lib/core/haptics/haptics.dart`, and `lib/core/speech/voice_input.dart` (no mic yet). To add another OneSpend widget, copy it the same way; don't hand-rewrite.

Floating chrome is OneSpend's, 1:1 (no liquid glass; it was tried and removed). Only the call screen uses `liquid_glass_widgets`, for its ColorOS 17 control motion (see `docs/call-screens.md`); the main app's own buttons (keypad keys, green Call and dialpad buttons, return-to-call banner, contact page pills) press with `PressGlow` (`lib/widgets/press_glow.dart`, the call screen's transparent `GlassButton.custom`: glow and stretch, no shader, so the call screen never calls `LiquidGlassWidgets.initialize`; the main engine calls it only once Settings › Display & effects › Glass & blur › Glass style is Liquid glass, see below), and the green ones are lit with the call screen's `solidBody`/`solidGlow` (`lib/features/in_call/call_controls.dart`):
- Every floating element (nav bar, header pills, scroll-to-top, action and selection bars, search pill, Cancel | Save, menus, modals) is a verbatim OneSpend widget on `OneUIGlassSurface`, in OneSpend's default flat look (the effects stand-in). Keep them verbatim: re-copy from `D:\one-spend\lib\widgets` rather than editing. Exceptions, restyled on purpose in the call screen's frosted "call glass" (`lib/widgets/call_glass.dart`: backdrop blur + `TintedCallBody` + `PressGlow`): the nav bar pill (`one_ui_floating_nav_bar.dart`; at Full effects, Glass style › Liquid glass swaps it for the package's `GlassTabBar.bottom` in the `GlassQuality` picked under Glass effect, and a tab's docked actions for a `GlassContainer` of the same glass, one sliding down as the other rises), the floating menu (`one_ui_floating_menu.dart`, which grows out of its button on a spring), and the Calls header (`round_header_controls.dart`, `_HeaderControls` in `recents_screen.dart`, which morphs between normal and selecting, and fades away while typing, when the keypad's number takes its place). Don't re-copy those. `one_ui_status_bar_scrim.dart` also differs from OneSpend: it's ColorOS 17's header blur (covers the Back button row, lighter tint, a light blur that `shaders/progressive_blur.frag` blurs more toward the top and fades out at the bottom (an `ImageFilter.shader`; a `ShaderMask` around a `BackdropFilter` cuts it off from the backdrop); the Calls and Contacts tabs host it as their `SliverAppBar.flexibleSpace`, behind the pills); don't re-copy it either. `one_ui_glass_surface.dart` has three dialer edits: it follows Settings › Reduce visual effects (grouped blur when reduced, solid fill when off) and Glass style › Liquid glass, and draws ColorOS 17 squircle corners; keep them when re-copying. Rounded rectangles everywhere use `SquircleBorder` (`lib/widgets/squircle.dart`, exported by `one_ui_theme.dart`; cards via `OneUITheme.cardShape`), which keeps circles and stadium pills exactly round: re-copied OneSpend widgets need their `RoundedRectangleBorder`/`ClipRRect`/`BoxDecoration` corners swapped for it, as in `one_ui_floating_modal.dart`. Glass style › Liquid glass (Settings › Display & effects › Glass & blur, `glass_settings_screen.dart`; Full effects only; the other style, our call glass, is labelled Edge glow, and its Glow strength row shows only in dark mode while some surface still uses it) swaps floating bodies for the package's `GlassContainer` (`liquidBody` in `lib/widgets/liquid_body.dart`), as far as "Liquid glass on" (`DialerPrefs.liquidScope`) reaches: the nav bar always (`isLiquid`), `CallGlass` and non-modal `OneUIGlassSurface`s unless Nav bar only (`liquidControls`), the floating menu and modal `OneUIGlassSurface`s only with Everything (`liquidPopups`); the keypad sheet and the call screen keep their own glass.
- `MainShell` hosts `OneUIFloatingNavBar` the way OneSpend's `home_screen.dart` does: in a `Stack` (the bar fills its parent's height, so it can't be a `Scaffold.bottomNavigationBar`), under a top `OneUIStatusBarScrim`. The bar always stays visible, including under the keypad sheet while typing. Tab taps use `Haptics.tap()`. While a tab is selecting, the bar morphs into its actions (`navDock` → `dockedContent`, a docked `OneUIFloatingActionBar`).
- Tabs pad with `navBarClearance()` (`lib/app/layout.dart`); bottom bars sit on one baseline (`kFloatingNavBarHeight`, `kNavBarVerticalPadding`).
- Selection mode follows OneSpend: the header's `isSelectionMode` with `OneUISelectionCountPill` (select all) and the cancel pill.
- Pages are ColorOS 17: `OneUISubpage` (and `CosSubpage`, a wrapper over it) is the round glass Back button + title over the header blur, and `OneUIGroupCard` is a flat grey card whose rows take a grey `value` and a chevron. The Calls and Contacts big titles hand over to a small title in the header bar as they scroll under it (`TitleCollapse`, `BigTitle`, `HeaderTitle` in `round_header_controls.dart`). Big actions use `GlowButton` (`lib/widgets/glow_button.dart`: the keypad Call button's lit body and `PressGlow`, or its call glass unlit), which shows a band of light sweeping across it while busy; Software update, About and Changelog show `VersionMark` (`lib/widgets/version_mark.dart`: the version in polished glass, then the name and channel) over `CosArtwork` (`lib/widgets/cos_artwork.dart`: sheets of silk drawn as threads of light, in the app's greens); both are painted once and cached.

## Working rules

- **Design language is ColorOS 17, not Samsung One UI.** The app was ported from OneSpend's One UI look (`D:\one-spend`); convert One UI leftovers (coloured icons, One UI fields, dialogs, popups, subpages) to the ColorOS 17 look when you touch them. ColorOS 16 screenshots the user shares are layout references, to be upgraded to the COS17 look (frosted call glass, springs).
- **The user tests on the phone.** After a change: `flutter analyze`, `flutter test`, then `flutter build apk --debug` and install it. Don't drive the phone with adb taps or screenshots to verify UI unless asked. Build `--release` only when the user asks (a release build can't install over a debug one, or the reverse, without uninstalling, which wipes the app's data: ask first). For motion changes (transitions, morphs) install a `--profile` build instead: debug draws too few frames to judge (profile has the fake "Test line" too).
- **Never install or force-stop the app during a call.** Every `adb install` or `am force-stop` must be conditional on every SIM being idle, with `&&`, never chained with `;` after a check that only prints:

  ```bash
  [ "$(adb shell dumpsys telephony.registry | grep -o 'mCallState=[0-9]' | sort -u)" = "mCallState=0" ] && adb install -r <apk> || echo "CALL IN PROGRESS - not installing"
  ```

  (0 = idle, 1 = ringing, 2 = off-hook.) An install during a call kills the InCallService and Android hands the call to another dialer or drops it. With two phones attached, pass `-s <serial>` to every adb command.
- **Release:** the app is prepared for Google Play closed testing (Crashlytics, Software update, `tool/release.ps1`). The upload key lives outside the repo; its path and passwords are only in the git-ignored `android/key.properties`. Never print, log or commit them.
- **Beta-only:** anything meant only for testing (Firebase Performance Monitoring, ...) gets a row in `docs/beta-only.md` in the same PR; that list is what comes out before the production release.
- **Two agents side by side:** work on separate branches, ideally in separate git worktrees; don't edit the same checkout at the same time.
- **Localization (English, Italian):** no user-facing literal in Dart or Kotlin. UI text is `context.l10n.key` from `lib/l10n/app_en.arb` + `app_it.arb` (add the key to both; `test/l10n_test.dart` checks parity); notification text is `res/values*/strings.xml` read through `AppLanguage.localized(context)`. The OneSpend-verbatim widgets' default labels (Cancel, Save, Search...) now come from `context.l10n`: keep that edit when re-copying one. Settings › Language and how to add a language: `docs/localization.md`; Italian still to be checked by a native speaker: `docs/l10n-review.md`.

## Git workflow

Remote: `https://github.com/syedarhamraza/one-dialer` (public, GPL-3.0; see `CONTRIBUTING.md`). Never commit to `main` or the beta branch directly. Never put real phone numbers, names or call logs in code, tests, commits or screenshots; use fakes like `0300 1234567`. The repo's default branch is always `main`, but no work is ever done on it: it holds only the last released beta.

- Each beta has its own long-lived branch cut from `main`, named `beta/<n>` (the current one is `beta/9`, for 1.0.0-beta.9). All work for that beta goes there: every change gets a branch (`feat/...`, `fix/...`, `docs/...`) cut from the beta branch, is pushed, and gets a pull request into the beta branch (`gh pr create --base beta/9`); merge only after the user approves.
- `main` only moves when a beta ships: once the user says the beta is released, the release commit (`tool/release.ps1`) goes on the beta branch, and the beta branch is merged into `main` with a pull request. Then cut `beta/<n+1>` from `main` and update the current branch name above.
- Pull requests opened against `main` (Dependabot and ImgBot use the default branch, and so do contributors who miss the note) get retargeted to the current beta branch (`gh pr edit <n> --base beta/9`) before they're merged.

## Commands

```bash
flutter pub get
flutter analyze
flutter test                                   # all tests
flutter test test/widget_test.dart             # one file
flutter test --plain-name "primary call"       # one test by name
flutter build apk --debug                      # also compiles the Kotlin layer
flutter run                                    # needs a real phone with a SIM for calls
flutter run --profile                          # judge smoothness here or in --release, never in debug (JIT)
flutter build apk --release                    # installs today: release falls back to debug signing without key.properties
```

Settings › **Reduce visual effects** (`full` / `reduced` / `off`, `DialerPrefs.visualEffects`) is read only through `OneUIEffectsController` (`isFull`, `isOff`, `oneUIFill`): full is the design as-is; reduced swaps the call glass for OneSpend's flat One UI fill over the blur and joins every blur on a page in one backdrop read (`MainShell` points its group at the page's); off is solid One UI fills with no blur at all. New glass or blurs should follow it too. Until the user picks a level, both settings start from the phone's chip (`DeviceTier.kt`, from `CallBridge.effectsDefault`: Off on budget GPUs, Reduced on midrange, Full above, low-RAM phones Off; unknown chips by GL renderer, then by RAM); why each chip sits where it does is in `docs/research/device-tiers/`, and Crashlytics keys `device_*` / `effects_*` show it per report. The call screen has its own setting, **Call screen effects** (`DialerPrefs.callEffects`, `callFlat(context)` under `InCallApp`'s `CallEffects`; `CallFrost` and the `flat:` variants of `solidBody`/`glassBody`/`glassGlow`), loaded unawaited in `inCallMain` and on resume. Benchmark and how to read it: `docs/performance.md`.

Performance rules that keep the lists smooth: list content goes in lazy slivers (`SliverList.builder`, `DecoratedSliver` for card backgrounds), never a `Column` of all rows. Selection taps update a `ValueNotifier` that only the selection circle (`SelectMark`) listens to. Row structure stays the same in and out of selection mode. Don't add new full-width `BackdropFilter`/`ShaderMask` layers beyond OneSpend's own and the keypad sheet's frosted blur (each is a save layer every frame). Blurs inside the shell's tabs (`CallGlass`, the keypad sheet) use `BackdropFilter.grouped` under `MainShell`'s `BackdropGroup`, so they share one backdrop read; anything that must blur another blur's result (the nav bar over the keypad) stays outside the group. Pushed pages get their own group from `DialerApp`'s page transition (and `RisePageRoute`). Liquid glass (Glass style › Liquid glass) starts its own backdrop group unless a `GlassBackdropGroup` is above it, so the same three places add one; keep liquid surfaces free of `Opacity`/`ShaderMask`/save-layer clips between them and it, or they read the backdrop alone. Don't put a `BackdropFilter` under an animated `Transform` (lay the glass out at the animated size instead, as the floating menu does), and keep `PressGlow`'s glow free of its mask blur: both cost tens of ms a frame on Adreno. Snapdragon (Adreno) phones pay the most for each separate backdrop read: Impeller's Vulkan path splits the render pass at every blur. Its GLES backend can't be forced in release (`--impeller-backend` is `#ifndef FLUTTER_RELEASE` in the engine), and the Skia opt-out was declined (deprecated), so fewer backdrop reads is the lever. Measure with `showPerformanceOverlay` in a `--profile` build (a local edit, never committed). The keypad keys draw their call glass from a cached image (`CallBodyArtwork`, no per-key blur), and the sheet is a `RepaintBoundary`.

`OneUIScrollPhysics` (the app-wide `OneUIScrollBehavior`) is a softened `BouncingScrollPhysics`: every page bounces at its ends, even empty or short ones, with a weaker pull (`frictionFactor`) and a stiffer return spring than Flutter's iOS bounce; big-header pages keep their snap. Fitted content that must hold still uses `HoldStillScrollPhysics` (a plain `ClampingScrollPhysics` would inherit the bounce's always-accept and let a held key drag the keypad): the keypad sheet, inner panels, and the call screen (the in-call engine uses the plain `MaterialScrollBehavior`). The shared `placeCall` SIM chooser is a `OneUIFloatingMenu` anchored to the tapped control.

Incoming calls can be tested without a second phone: debug and profile builds include a fake "Test line" (`android/app/src/debug/.../FakeCalls.kt`; `build.gradle.kts` adds it to profile; never in release). Enable it once, then ring the phone (add `--ez private true` for a hidden number):

```bash
adb shell telecom set-phone-account-enabled com.syedarhamraza.onedialer/com.syedarhamraza.dialer.debug.FakeCallService fake 0
adb shell am broadcast -n com.syedarhamraza.onedialer/com.syedarhamraza.dialer.debug.FakeCallReceiver --es number 03001234567
```

Call flows only work after the app is set as the default phone app (the home screen's "Set" button). Emulators can only fake calls (`adb emu gsm call <number>`).

## Architecture

Two Flutter engines run in one app, sharing the same Kotlin call state:

- **Main engine** (`main()` in `lib/main.dart` → `DialerApp`) is hosted by `MainActivity`: dial pad, recents, contacts, default-dialer setup.
- **In-call engine** (`inCallMain()`, `@pragma('vm:entry-point')`) is created and cached by `InCallEngine.kt` right after `DialerInCallService.onCallAdded` posts the ringing notification (never before it: building the engine takes ~0.5 s on a cold start), then hosted by `InCallActivity` (lock-screen capable, `singleInstance`). It's pre-warmed so the call screen opens without a cold start, and it outlives the activity (`shouldDestroyEngineWithHost = false`).

Call flow (Kotlin, `android/app/src/main/kotlin/com/syedarhamraza/dialer/`):

- `telecom/DialerInCallService` gets `onCallAdded`/`onCallRemoved` from Telecom. Ringing calls → `CallNotifications` full-screen CallStyle notification (the only reliable way to show UI from the background); other calls → `InCallActivity.start`.
- `telecom/CallManager` (singleton object) is the single source of truth: assigns string ids (`call-N`) to `android.telecom.Call` objects, registers callbacks, and serializes everything to `{calls: [...], audio: {...}}` via `snapshot()`. Every change calls `notifyChanged()` → listeners.
- `CallBridge` is created once per engine and exposes the method channel `com.syedarhamraza.dialer/telecom` plus the event channel `com.syedarhamraza.dialer/call_state` (which streams `CallManager.snapshot()` on every change). Role requests are delegated to `MainActivity` (it owns the activity result); the in-call engine's bridge has no `roleRequester`.
- `telecom/CallActionReceiver` handles the notification's Decline/Hang up buttons. Answer is an activity `PendingIntent` (`CallActionReceiver.answerIntent`, answered in `InCallActivity`): Android 12+ blocks activity starts from a notification's broadcast, so keep it that way. The ongoing-call channel is not `IMPORTANCE_LOW` (silent ones are hidden on the lock screen).
- On vivo (OriginOS), `CallNotifications` adds vivo's Atomic Island extras (`notification.superx.*`) to the ringing notification, so Origin Island shows a card with Decline/Answer; while unlocked it posts on a quieter channel so Android's banner doesn't double it. Answering on the card keeps the call in the island: the ongoing notification becomes an in-call card (Speaker toggle via `CallActionReceiver.ACTION_SPEAKER`, Hang up) and drops CallStyle on vivo, which would add vivo's own call island. vivo drops island updates closer than 10 s apart, so the ongoing one reposts only when its look changes. Other Android 16 phones get a Live Update request instead. The card's images (and the launcher and Play icons) are rendered by `python tool/icons/render_icons.py` into `res/drawable-nodpi/`; the status bar icons (`ic_stat_*.xml`) use the same rounded handset.
- There is one launcher icon, `ic_launcher` (also the Play icon), on the `PhoneIcon` activity-alias, so `MainActivity` has no LAUNCHER filter and holds the contact VIEW/EDIT/INSERT filters itself; `AppIcons.repair` turns it back on for people updating from a version that had Settings › App icon. The Contacts icon and the Classic style were dropped on purpose: vivo's Colored and Dark icon styles draw one picture per package, so a second icon showed the first one's picture. vivo's Dark style tints the foreground green only when the glyph is plain geometry with a top-to-bottom gloss and a drop shadow (like One Recorder's); filter-thinned glyphs stay white. It also caches those renders by resource ID and never refreshes them, so `android/app/launcher-icon-ids.txt` (aapt2 `--stable-ids`, from `build.gradle.kts`) pins `ic_launcher` to its own ID: when the icon's design changes, give it a new, never-used ID (and rebuild with `--rerun-tasks`, since Gradle doesn't track that file). `render_icons.py` renders it, its flat monochrome (themed icon) vector and `assets/app_icons/default_phone.png` (the website's icon). Draw icons from the app's own glyphs, never traced from another OS (Play's IP policy).
- `InCallActivity` finishes itself when `CallManager` has no calls left.
- The whole app is designed at 420 dp wide (the vivo it was tuned on): `DesignWidth` in `lib/app/layout.dart`, the `MaterialApp.builder` of both engines (`DialerApp` and `InCallApp`), lays it out at that width and scales it to the screen (down on a 360 dp Galaxy S25, up to 1.15× on wider phones and tablets; landscape unscaled), so every size in `lib/` is in vivo dp. Tune on the vivo; don't add per-device breakpoints. `test/in_call_screen_test.dart` checks the proportions at common screen sizes (with `PREVIEW_FONT` it writes `build/call-previews/sizes/`); on a real phone, `adb shell wm density <dpi>` simulates other widths (`wm density reset` after).
- `InCallActivity` uses `InCallTheme`, an opaque black window; the call screen's backdrop is Flutter's own gradient aurora tinted by the caller (`CallBackdrop`), not the system wallpaper (a translucent wallpaper window flashed the home screen or black while opening on vivo).

Main UI: `DialerApp` gates on the dialer role (`SetupScreen` until it's granted; `CallBridge` watches the call log only once `READ_CALL_LOG` arrives with the role, so launching without it doesn't crash), then `MainShell` (`lib/app/main_shell.dart`) shows the Calls (Recents) / Contacts tabs under `OneUIFloatingNavBar`, with `KeypadScreen` as a sheet over Calls (offstage on Contacts). `ContactsRepository.instance` (`lib/core/contacts/`, built on `flutter_contacts`) holds contacts for T9 search (`lib/features/keypad/t9.dart`, unit-tested in `test/t9_test.dart`). Recents reads the system call log through `CallLogSource.kt` (a `ContentObserver` in `CallBridge` pushes `onCallLogChanged`) into `CallLogRepository` (`lib/features/recents/`); grouping and labels are unit-tested in `test/call_log_test.dart`. Missed-call notifications are ours to post as default dialer (`MissedCallReceiver.kt`, built from the call log, never from the exported broadcast's extras). Call-log `simId`s use the same `component|accountId` form as `getSims()`. Contacts (`lib/features/contacts/`) read `ContactsRepository`; the tab follows the Calls tab's look (pinned black header with glass `RoundIconButton`s, big title, favourites under ★, flat fixed-extent rows, ColorOS A–Z letter index; tap opens details, long-press selects) and (`DialerContact` extends T9's entry, numbers deduped by `numberKey`); add, edit and pick use Android's own contact screens via `flutter_contacts`' `native` API. Speed dial and per-contact SIM live in `DialerPrefs` (`shared_preferences`). Contacts ⋮ › Manage contacts (`manage_contacts_screen.dart`) sets `DialerPrefs`' contact choices (photos, numberless contacts, hidden accounts, sort and name format, save location); `ContactsRepository.contacts` keeps only contacts with numbers (T9, search, calls), `allContacts` has the rest. Import uses the native `pickVcf`, SIM contacts `FlutterContacts.sim`, and Merge duplicates `ContactExtras.merge` (`AggregationExceptions` keep-together). Other apps' contact VIEW/EDIT/INSERT intents open here (`MainActivity.contactActionFrom` → `onContactAction` → `MainShell._contactAction`). Every call from a list or details page goes through `placeCall()` in `lib/widgets/place_call.dart`: explicit SIM, else the contact's SIM, else our SIM sheet on dual-SIM "Ask every time". Settings (`lib/features/settings/`) shows a health check from `getCallHealth` (default dialer, notifications, full-screen intent, screening role) with fix buttons. Quick decline messages live in native prefs (`QuickReplies.kt`) so the in-call engine reads the same list. Settings › Software update and its Changelog are ported from OneSpend (`lib/features/updates/`, `lib/core/config/changelog.dart`); Play gives no release notes, so an offered update's notes come from the website's `one-dialer/changelog.json`, which `tool/release.ps1` writes to `site/public/changelog.json` (`tool/export_changelog.dart`) in the release commit; Crashlytics starts in both engines (`lib/core/crash_reporting.dart`) and is off without `android/app/google-services.json`. Release: `tool/release.ps1` (signing via `android/key.properties`) and `docs/privacy-policy.md`; keep both in sync with the manifest's permissions. The website (`syedarhamraza.github.io/one-dialer/`, the app's privacy and release-notes URLs) is `site/` (Next.js static export; named `site` because Flutter reserves `web/`), deployed by `.github/workflows/pages.yml`. `.github/workflows/release.yml` (run by hand, approved in the `release` environment) runs `tool/release.ps1` with the draft GitHub release's lines as the notes (Release Drafter builds that draft from merged pull requests, labelled by branch prefix: `.github/release-drafter.yml`), uploads to Play and publishes the recorder APK on GitHub Releases. Pull request titles are the first draft of the release notes, so write them for users.

Blocking (`lib/features/blocking/`): the block list is Android's system list via `flutter_contacts`' `blockedNumbers` (Telecom rejects those numbers itself); the private/non-contact rules run in `DialerScreeningService.kt`, read from native SharedPreferences (`BlockingPrefs`), with the decision in `BlockRules` (JVM tests: `cd android && ./gradlew :app:testDebugUnitTest`; results land in `build/app/test-results/`). Android only binds the **call-screening role** holder's screening service (being the default dialer isn't enough), so enabling a rule requests that role. SIMs come from `TelecomBridge.getSims()` (real SIM accounts only); the keypad uses the shared SIM chooser when there's no default SIM.

`android/gradle.properties` sets `kotlin.incremental=false`: the pub cache (C:) and the project (D:) are on different drives, and incremental Kotlin compilation of plugins fails across them.

Keypad layout: `KeypadScreen` is a frosted, rounded sheet (drag handle, call-glass stadium-pill keys, bottom row dialpad | green Call | delete) over the Calls list; the shell's `open` notifier says whether it's up (`tel:` links open it, the missed-call notification closes it). With nothing typed it draws no backdrop, so Calls shows and takes taps; while typing, the typed number takes the Calls header's place at the top (an editable `TextField` that never opens the phone's keyboard: tap for a cursor the keys type at, hold for the system's Cut/Copy/Paste; grouped as you type by libphonenumber's `AsYouTypeFormatter` through `formatAsYouType`), with the T9 results under it, or New contact / Add to existing contact when nothing matches. Holding 0 types +, * a pause (,) and # a wait (;). The handle, the dialpad pill, Android Back or scrolling the results hide it, leaving a green dialpad button beside the nav bar; that button or a picked result brings it back, and the typed number is kept. Its `isActive` flag prevents the offstage keypad from intercepting Back. Small/landscape screens scroll inside the sheet.

The keypad has one centered cellular call button. `placeCall` uses the contact's saved SIM or the system default, otherwise shows the shared SIM chooser; its boolean result lets the keypad retain the typed number when selection is cancelled. The sheet's bottom keeps navigation-bar clearance, so the keys sit above the bar and don't shift while typing. A 300 ms slide moves the sheet down and up, honoring reduced-motion settings. The experimental keypad WhatsApp calling integration was removed; existing contact-page WhatsApp chat actions are unchanged. Optional visual review: `flutter test test/keypad_screen_test.dart --dart-define=PREVIEW_FONT=<Flutter SDK>/bin/cache/artifacts/material_fonts/roboto-regular.ttf` writes previews to `build/`.

Dart side: `lib/core/telecom/telecom_bridge.dart` (`TelecomBridge.instance`) mirrors `CallBridge.kt`, and `call_info.dart` parses the snapshot maps (`CallSnapshot`, `CallInfo`, `AudioInfo`). **Channel names, method names, argument keys and the state/route strings must stay in sync** between `CallBridge.kt`/`CallManager.kt` and these two Dart files.

The ringtone is played by Telecom, not the app: `IN_CALL_SERVICE_RINGING` is deliberately left out of `AndroidManifest.xml`. Add it only together with the app's own ringtone and vibration.

Audio routing and `onCallAudioStateChanged` use the `CallAudioState` APIs, which are deprecated on API 34+ but still work. They're kept for minSdk 29 compatibility.

`graphify` is the pipx package `graphifyy` (it may not be on PATH). The graph covers project code only; `.agents/` and `.claude/` skill files are deliberately excluded.

Rules:

- When graphify-out/graph.json exists, run `graphify query "<question>"` before your first grep, search or source-file read for a codebase question, and put this rule in every subagent prompt that explores code. Read raw files only for lines the graph or an earlier read already located. Use `graphify path "<A>" "<B>"` for relationships and `graphify explain "<concept>"` for focused concepts. These return a scoped subgraph, usually much smaller than GRAPH_REPORT.md or raw grep output.
- If graphify-out/wiki/index.md exists, use it for broad navigation instead of raw source browsing.
- Read graphify-out/GRAPH_REPORT.md only for broad architecture review or when query/path/explain do not surface enough context.
- After modifying code, run `graphify update .` to keep the graph current (AST-only, no API cost).
- A committed graphify-out/graph.json is rewritten whole on every build, so a large diff to it in a pull request is expected. Do not flag it as churn or suggest skipping the graph rebuild or its hooks.

## Call recording (companion app)

Recording is a separate sideloaded app, **One Recorder** (`companion/`, plain Kotlin, `com.syedarhamraza.onedialer.recorder`), never Play: it holds the mic and an empty accessibility service, and One Dialer drives it through its provider's `call()` (`telecom/RecorderCompanion.kt`; all recording UI hides when it isn't installed). It trusts One Dialer by pinned signing certificate (`companion/.../TrustedDialer.kt`): a release companion only the Play app-signing key (so it works only with Play installs of One Dialer), a debug companion also the upload and debug keys. Build and test it with `companion/gradlew.bat -p companion test assembleDebug` (needs `companion/local.properties` with `sdk.dir`), and install `companion/app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk` with the same call-idle gate. Design and what's still unverified: `docs/call-recording-plan.md`.

## Liquid Glass Widgets

For any liquid glass work, read the package's own guide first: `skills/liquid-glass-widgets/SKILL.md` inside the `liquid_glass_widgets` package. Its folder is that package's `rootUri` in `.dart_tool/package_config.json` (after `flutter pub get`), so it always matches the version in `pubspec.lock`. Don't copy it into this repo.

In this repo, these rules come before the package guide:

- Only the call screen uses the package's controls (`GlassButton.custom`, for the ColorOS 17 control motion). The main app's buttons press with `PressGlow` and never call `LiquidGlassWidgets.initialize`, except the main engine once Settings › Display & effects › Glass & blur › Glass style is Liquid glass.
- Liquid glass is opt-in and Full-effects only. Floating bodies switch to `GlassContainer` through `liquidBody` (`lib/widgets/liquid_body.dart`), as far as `DialerPrefs.liquidScope` reaches (`isLiquid`, `liquidControls`, `liquidPopups`). Reduced and Off never show liquid glass, and the keypad sheet and the call screen keep their own glass.
- The nav bar's liquid form is `GlassTabBar.bottom` in the `GlassQuality` picked under Glass effect, not a hard-coded quality.
- Each place hosting several liquid surfaces (the shell's tabs, pushed pages, `RisePageRoute`) adds a `GlassBackdropGroup`, so they share one backdrop read. Keep `Opacity`, `ShaderMask` and save-layer clips out from between a liquid surface and its group, or it reads the backdrop alone.
- No liquid surface under an animated `Transform`; lay it out at the animated size instead, as the floating menu does.
